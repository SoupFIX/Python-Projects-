## 2. Data Cleaning Log

The initial dataset underwent several critical cleaning actions to enhance its quality, consistency, and suitability for analysis. Each step was meticulously performed to ensure data integrity:

*   **Initial State**: The dataset began with **891 rows** and **12 columns**.
*   **Duplicate Row Check**: No duplicate rows were found, ensuring each record represents a unique passenger entry. This is crucial for accurate statistical analysis, preventing overcounting or biased results.
*   **Column Renaming**: Columns were standardized to lowercase snake_case (e.g., `PassengerId` to **passengerid**, `Survived` to **survived**). This improves readability and consistency, making column referencing easier and less error-prone in programming environments.
*   **Missing Value Imputation (Age)**: **177** missing values in the **age** column were filled with the median value of **28.00**. This strategy is preferred over mean imputation for skewed distributions and helps retain a significant portion of the dataset (approximately **19.86%** of age data) that would otherwise be discarded, allowing for more robust demographic analysis.
*   **Column Dropping (Cabin)**: The 'cabin' column was dropped due to a high percentage of missing values (**77%**). Such a high proportion of missing data makes the column unreliable for analysis and its removal prevents noise and potential misinterpretations.
*   **Missing Value Imputation (Embarked)**: **2** missing values in the **embarked** column were filled with 'Unknown'. This ensures that all records have a value for this categorical feature, preventing errors in categorical analysis and allowing all passengers to be included in analyses related to embarkation port.
*   **Outlier Capping (Numerical Columns)**:
    *   **passengerid**, **survived**, **pclass**: No outliers were capped, indicating these columns were within expected ranges or had no values considered extreme based on the defined capping thresholds. This confirms the integrity of these fundamental identifiers and categorical variables.
    *   **age**: **11** outliers were capped within the range of **[-6.7-64.8]**. This process limits extreme values, preventing them from disproportionately influencing statistical models and ensuring that age distributions are more representative of the general passenger population.
    *   **sibsp**: **46** outliers were capped within the range of **[-1.5-2.5]**. This addresses extreme values for siblings/spouses, making the family size variable more consistent and reducing the impact of rare, very large family groups.
    *   **parch**: **213** outliers were capped within the range of **[0.0-0.0]**. This is a significant action, effectively setting all values in **parch** to **0.00**. This transformation implies a decision to treat all passengers as having no parents or children aboard for the purpose of this analysis, or that any non-zero values were deemed outliers and reset. This drastically alters the meaning of the column and should be noted for its impact on family-related insights.
    *   **fare**: **116** outliers were capped within the range of **[-26.7-65.6]**. Capping extreme fare values helps to normalize the distribution of ticket prices, preventing a few very expensive tickets from skewing analyses related to economic status or class.
*   **Whitespace Stripping**: Whitespace was removed from **4 text columns**. This standardizes text entries, preventing issues with string matching and ensuring consistency in categorical data.
*   **Final State**: The cleaning process concluded with **891 rows** and **11 columns**, resulting in a refined dataset ready for in-depth analysis.

## 6. Recommendations

Based on the analysis of the dataset, summary statistics, and implied insights from the data cleaning and visualizations, the following actionable recommendations are proposed for further investigation and business strategy:

*   **Deep Dive into Passenger Class (pclass) and Survival**: Given that a significant majority of passengers were in **3rd class** (median **3.00**), a detailed analysis of survival rates across **pclass** is crucial. Investigate if **pclass** is a strong predictor of survival and explore the underlying reasons (e.g., access to lifeboats, cabin location). This can inform future safety protocols or emergency planning for large vessels, emphasizing equitable access and procedures.
*   **Analyze Age-Specific Survival Rates**: The wide range of **age** (from **0.42** to **64.81** years) suggests that age could be a significant factor in survival. Segment passengers into distinct age groups (e.g., infants/children, young adults, middle-aged, elderly) and analyze their respective survival rates. This could reveal if specific age demographics were prioritized or disadvantaged during the evacuation, informing future emergency training and resource allocation.
*   **Re-evaluate and Re-process the 'Parch' Column**: The aggressive capping of the **parch** column to **0.00** for all entries has rendered it uninformative for family analysis. It is strongly recommended to revisit the original 'parch' data, understand the rationale behind the capping, and if possible, re-process it with a less restrictive outlier strategy (e.g., capping at a reasonable maximum like 2 or 3, or using a different imputation method for missing values). This would enable valuable insights into the impact of traveling with parents or children on survival outcomes.
*   **Investigate Fare-Survival Relationship and its Link to Class**: The high variability in **fare** paid, coupled with its likely correlation to **pclass**, warrants further investigation. Analyze how **fare** directly correlates with survival, and then examine this relationship within each **pclass**. This could reveal if higher-paying passengers (even within the same class) had better survival chances, providing insights into socio-economic factors influencing outcomes.
*   **Leverage Titles from 'Name' for Social Status Analysis**: Assuming the `04_top_categories_name.png` chart highlights passenger titles (e.g., Mr., Mrs., Miss, Master), it is recommended to extract and categorize these titles into a new feature. This new 'Title' feature can serve as a proxy for social status, marital status, or age group, and can be used to analyze its correlation with survival.
